Every year, journalists and researchers uncover "redacted" documents where the sensitive information is fully readable. The reason: black boxes were placed on top of text, not replacing it. Real redaction permanently destroys the underlying data. Here's how to do it correctly.

What to redact (common use cases)

  • Legal documents: Names of protected witnesses or minors, sealed case information.
  • Financial records: Account numbers, sort codes, card numbers.
  • Medical records: Patient names, NHS/insurance numbers, diagnoses.
  • HR documents: Salary information, personal addresses, national insurance numbers.
  • Freedom of Information responses: Commercially sensitive or personally identifiable information.

Also remove hidden metadata

PDF files can contain hidden metadata: author name, organisation, creation software, edit history, and comments. When sharing a redacted document, it's good practice to also strip this metadata.

Frequently asked questions

Can I redact images as well as text?

Yes. The redaction selection box works on any content — text, images, signatures, logos, or any other visual element.

Does PDFQuix's redaction work on scanned PDFs?

Yes. For scanned PDFs (image-based), the redaction tool removes the selected region from the image itself, not just a text layer.

Can I undo redaction?

No — that's the point of proper redaction. Once you download the redacted file, the content is permanently gone from that file. This is why we recommend keeping the original separately.
