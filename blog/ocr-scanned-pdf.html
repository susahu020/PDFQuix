How to Make a Scanned PDF Searchable with OCR

✍️ PDFQuix Team 📅 April 2025 ⏱️ 6 min read

Scanned PDFs are digital images, not text. You can read them on screen, but you can't search for a word, select text to copy, or convert them to Word. OCR (Optical Character Recognition) fixes all of that in one click.

How to know if your PDF is scanned

Try selecting text in the PDF. If you can highlight individual words by clicking and dragging, the PDF contains real text. If clicking creates a selection box over the entire page (like selecting an image), it's a scanned PDF with no real text layer.

ℹ️ Another test: Press Ctrl+F (or Cmd+F on Mac) to search. If the search finds nothing even for words clearly visible on screen, the PDF is image-only and needs OCR.

How OCR works

OCR software analyses the shapes of letters and words in an image, compares them against known character patterns, and outputs the corresponding text. Modern OCR (like Tesseract, which PDFQuix uses) uses machine learning to recognise characters with high accuracy even in varied fonts.

The result is a PDF with two layers: the original image (which still looks the same) and an invisible text layer underneath, enabling search, copy, and text-to-speech.

Frequently asked questions

Can OCR work on PDFs in languages other than English?

Yes. PDFQuix's OCR supports 100+ languages including Hindi, Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, French, German, and Spanish. Select the correct language before processing for best results.

Does OCR alter the visual appearance of the PDF?

No. The original scanned images remain unchanged. OCR adds an invisible text layer underneath.

What if some pages are scanned and some have real text?

PDFQuix applies OCR only to image-based pages and leaves existing text pages untouched.
