Why are PDFs so large?

The most common culprits are:

  • Unoptimised images — A high-resolution photo embedded at print quality (300 dpi) is massive. For screen viewing, 96–150 dpi is more than enough.
  • Embedded fonts — Some tools embed every character in a font, not just the ones used. This can add megabytes.
  • Hidden layers and metadata — PDFs can contain invisible content, edit history, and author metadata that bloats the file.
  • Scanned pages — A scanned document saved as a series of raw JPEG images inside a PDF is enormous without compression.

How much smaller will my PDF get?

It depends heavily on what's inside your PDF:

  • Scanned documents — Often 50–80% reduction.
  • Reports with photos — Usually 40–70% reduction.
  • Text-only PDFs — Often only 5–20% reduction (text is already small).
  • Already-compressed PDFs — Very little reduction (already optimised).

Frequently asked questions

Does compressing a PDF reduce quality?

Text and vector graphics are completely lossless — they look identical at any compression level. Images may lose some crispness at high compression, but the Medium setting preserves visible quality for screen and email use.

Is there a file size limit?

PDFQuick imposes no file size limit. The only limit is your device's RAM. For very large files (500 MB+), we recommend closing other browser tabs first.

Do you upload my file?

No. All compression runs using JavaScript in your browser. Your file never leaves your device and we never see its contents.
